# Sinclair ZX80 (Issue 2 board)

The board being checked against this netlist is a Sinclair ZX80 **Issue 2**. The circuit comes from the ZX80 schematic and the
placement from a scan of an Issue 3 board, so neither has been checked against the Issue 2 yet: the checks are done with a meter
on the real board, one at a time, as for the TS1000 and TS1500.

`--machine zx80`. Z80A, 4K ROM (8332), 1K RAM (2 x 2114), discrete TTL: no ULA. The display is made by the CPU running the
display file with A15 high while IC14/IC15 force NOPs; /INT is wired to A6 and ends the HALT of every display line.

ROMs (not shipped): `roms/zx80/*.rom` (4096 bytes, the ZX80's) and `roms/ts1000/*.rom` (8192 bytes, the ZX81's). Choose with
`--option rom=zx80` or `rom=zx81`. With the 8K ROM pin 21 of IC2 is A12; the ZX81 ROM runs in FAST mode only.

The keyboard (option `keyboard=zx80|zx81`, or the Keyboard button in the control panel; independent of the ROM choice) is by default the ZX80's: the ZX81's matrix but its own shifted keys (SHIFT+1..4 NOT AND THEN TO, +5..8 cursors, +9 HOME, +0 RUBOUT, +NEWLINE EDIT, +Y U I O P is " $ ( ) *), from a scan of the keyboard. The ZX80 ROM is not the ZX81 ROM, so with the ZX81 ROM the keywords differ from the legends.

The netlist is `tools/zx80-netlist-src/` (built from the PDF with `tools/zx80-extract/`). The crystal is not simulated (the
machine drives IC20's amplifier, and stops it when X1 is bad, removed or open) and C11 is a pulse made once per phi rising edge. The power supply, modulator, EAR/MIC and edge
connector are not modeled. Parts are placed from a photo of the Issue 2 board (edge connector at the top), every part by its silkscreen label; R36, R37, R26 and D1, D2 are empty footprints on this board and not drawn. The keyboard is a membrane: KB1 and KB2 stand for its two ribbon contacts and are drawn below the board.

Faults: X1 (the crystal) can be made bad, removed or open: the oscillator stops, the Z80 stays in reset and there is no picture. `tests/zx80-faults.sh` lists the faults with what the circuit says they should do (not checked on a real board).
