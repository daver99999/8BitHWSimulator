# Timex Sinclair 1500

`--machine ts1500`, or Timex > TS1500 in the controls.

The TS1500 is not a TS1000 with 16K of RAM. The ZX81's 40 pin ULA is replaced by **U5, a custom 68 pin gate array**
(an NCR F811592 ULA on the schematic) that also runs the memory: eight 4116 DRAMs (U8-U15, 16K x 1) with /RAS, /CAS, /WE and
the multiplexed address RA0-RA7, and a 74LS245 data transceiver (U6) and 74LS244 address buffer (U3) switched by U5's /DEN
and /AEN. The ROM (U2, 8K) is the TS1500's own.

## How good is this netlist?

It is transcribed from the schematic **"TS-1500 Rev 1.0, 2/3/2015, redrawn by Dick Berry"** (a 1 page PDF), which prints the pin
number of every U5 pin, every chip pin and the ref of every part. The refs are the real board's (checked against photos of a
real board): the Z80 is **U7**, the keyboard connectors are **J5** (KBD COLS) and **J6** (KBD ROWS), the eight keyboard diodes
are **D10-D17**, the 1K in the ROM's A8 line is **R28**, the 680 in the /ROMCS line is **R25**.

Checked with continuity on a real board (Z80 pin to U5 pin): /M1 2, /NMI 3, /IORQ 4, /RD 5, /WR 6, /MREQ 7, /HALT 8, /WAIT 9,
/RESET 10, /RFSH 16. All agree with the schematic.

What the schematic says that earlier guesses had wrong: U6's DIR is on **/WR**, not /RD; the edge connector's data contacts are on the
primed bus (D0'-D7'); there is no separate "CLKN" pin (the Z80's CLK is on pin 58, SYS Clk, checked on a real board; the schematic says 57).

Still open (marked in `tools/ts1500-netlist-src/sheet01.py`):
- where U5 pin **57** (/CPU Clock) goes (the Z80's CLK is on 58), (the edge connector's SYS Clk contact, the 6th on the underside counting the key gap from the +5V end, beeps to Z80 pin 6: checked, so P1.12 is on the clock net);
- pin 14 (/RAMCS: checked, not connected to the Z80 or the DRAMs; where else it goes is not known) and pin 21 (60/50 Hz, goes to the board's open "50 60" jumper pad, checked; 4.1K to GND and 2.75K to +5V in circuit) are not modeled (pin 18 is on GND, checked);
- the parts are placed from a photo of the board (tools/ts1500-netlist-src/placement.py); the small parts and the diode stacks are put where the photo has one of that kind, not by silkscreen label. The buck regulator and wires added to the board are mods and are not modeled.

Not transcribed: the linear and switching power supplies (the DRAMs' -5V and +12V have no source here), Q1's pin order.

Power off. Z80 U7 pin to the real U5 pin (the schematic's number in brackets). The ones already checked are listed above.

U5 outputs to the other chips: /DEN 12 to U6 pin 19, /AEN 13 to U3 pins 1 and 19, /ROMCS 15 to R25 and then ROM pin 20, RA0-RA6

modulator, OSC-OUT 55 and OSC-IN 56 to the crystal X1.

## Model

- **U5** is the ZX81 ULA's behaviour (`src/ula81.c`, now taking a pin table) plus the DRAM timing: /RAS when /MREQ and A14
  and not a refresh, /CAS three half periods after the access (/RD or /WR) starts, RA0-RA6 the row (A0-A6) and then the
  column (A7-A13). The WAIT line (the TS1000 makes it with transistor TR1) is made inside U5.
- **Assumed, not known:** the character bitmap path in a video fetch (read from the ROM's outputs while U6 is switched off), the keyboard pull-ups (inside U5 here),
  DRAM refresh (not simulated: the model keeps its data), the +12V and -5V of the DRAMs (their pins are unchecked).
- 16K RAM at $4000-$7FFF. The ROM finds RAMTOP = $8000.
- The ROM boots slowly (about 4 seconds of emulated time to the K cursor): it clears and expands the display file.
- ROM: put the 8K TS1500 ROM (`.rom`, `.bin` or `.u2`, 8192 bytes) in `roms/ts1500`. It is not shipped.
- Not modeled: the power section (the 5V regulator, the -5V/+12V converter), the modulator and the video levels, the
  tape circuits, the expansion pack, the TV channel select keys.
- Headless check: boots to the K cursor and runs `PRINT 1` (`0/0` report at the bottom).

## Keyboard rows (checked on a real board)

J6 pins 1-8 go through the diodes D13, D17, D10, D16, D12, D14, D15, D11 to the Z80 address lines A11, A10, A9, A12, A13, A8, A14, A15 (Z80 pins 1, 40, 39, 2, 3, 38, 4, 5). The schematic draws a different order for six of them; the board was measured, so the netlist follows the board.
