# Changelog

What changed in each release of 8bit-hw-simulator, newest first.
Changes not yet in a release are under "Unreleased".

This project is AI-generated: the code and these notes were written with
Claude (Anthropic's AI), directed and tested by zzleezz (and, for the Commodore
PET, by David Green, github.com/daver99999). It started as antirez's
[c64-hw-simulator](https://github.com/antirez/c64-hw-simulator).

## Unreleased

- **TS1000/ZX81 Issue 1 board (Timex tab).** `--machine ts1000i1`, or the Timex tab's board choice. The same SRC 049 circuit as the Issue 3, with the Issue 1's resistor packs RP1 (data lines) and RP3 (keyboard columns) in place of R35-R47, and the parts placed from the Issue 1 layout drawing (Dave Briggs, spectrumforeveryone.com), edge connector at the top. Generated by `tools/ts1000i1_netlist2c.py`. Which pack pin is on which line, and the keyboard connector KB2's pin order, are inferred and still to be checked against a real board.

- **TS1000/ZX81 fixes.** A second key press froze the CPU for good: the /WAIT transistor TR1 was not re-evaluated when /NMI went high. The monitor now loses the picture when the TV (modulator) pin is broken or the ULA stops making frames (a dark screen, as on the real board), and shows snow only when there is no signal. Headless typing on the ZX81 needs `C64HW_KEYFRAMES` (the ROM debounces keys); `tests/ts1000.sh` covers boot and typing.

- **MC-10 (Radio Shack tab): first release.** `--machine mc10` or `--board mc10`, or the Radio Shack tab's board choice next to the Color Computer 2. Built at pin level from the Eagle schematic of the MC-10, read by the new `tools/mc10_sch2c.py`. New models: a cycle accurate MC6803 core (`chips/m6803.h`, `src/m6803.c`: the 6801 instruction set, the on-chip registers and 128 bytes of RAM, interrupts, WAI, MUL), the plain MC6847 reading the board's own RAM addresses (`PT_6847_MC10`), the 74LS155, 74LS367, 74LS76, MC14503 and the 2564 ROM, with the board's 74LS74 clock stretch and !WIN write window simulated chip by chip. It boots to the Microcolor BASIC `OK` prompt and takes the keyboard (capitals without Shift, Esc = BREAK; Shift plus a letter makes a semigraphics block on this ROM). Notes: the schematic has no layout, so the parts are placed where a top-view photo of the board shows them (the chips are all drawn pin 1 at the top left, and U12, U13 and U20 are matched to the photo by the silkscreen: a guess for U12, which is not visible); the schematic wires the 6803's A12 pin to net A11, which the converter patches. Put `mc10.rom` in `roms/mc10` (see the README). Not built: cassette, sound, the serial port and the expansion port.

- **Dragon 64 (Tano tab): first release.** `--machine dragon64` or `--board dragon64`, or the new Tano tab. Built at pin level from the issue 6 board, whose netlist comes from the KiCad PCB of the Dragon schematics project (through `tools/kicad_pcb2c.py`). It reuses the CoCo 2's 6809E, PIAs and 4164s, and adds the 6883 SAM (with the VCLK output), the plain 6847 (it counts its own CLK pin, and reads the RAM's byte from the 74LS273 latch the board puts between the RAM and the video chip), the 6551 ACIA, a real 74LS74, 74LS273, 74LS393 and 74LS390. The PAL timing circuit of the board (the counters and gates that stop the 6847's clock to stretch its 262 lines to the 312 of 50 Hz) is simulated chip by chip: the frame rate comes out at 50 Hz. It boots to the Dragon BASIC banner and takes the keyboard (the Dragon's matrix, found by pressing every crossing). This is the PAL board: the Tano Dragon was an NTSC Dragon 64, which is not drawn. Not built: the analog chips (sound, joysticks, the PAL colour encoder), cassette, serial port, printer, cartridge and the disk.

- **Color Computer 2 (Radio Shack tab): first release.** `--machine coco2` or `--board coco2`, or the new Radio Shack tab. Built at pin level from the 26-3134B/3136B board, whose netlist comes from the KiCad PCB of the coco_motherboards project through the new `tools/kicad_pcb2c.py` (it reads the pads and their nets straight from a PCB, which the schematic tracer could not do for a schematic of nested sheets; `tools/pcbparts.py` says which chip is which model). New chip models: a cycle accurate MC6809E core written for this simulator (`chips/m6809.h`, `src/m6809.c`: the whole instruction set with its real timings, interrupts, HALT, SYNC, CWAI), the 74LS785 (MC6883) SAM (the memory map, the E and Q clocks, /RAS /CAS /W and the multiplexed address of the two RAM accesses in each cycle, the video address counter with its X and Y dividers) and the MC6847T1 (it draws the picture from the bytes the SAM's counter makes the RAM give it, and is also the transceiver between the CPU's data bus and the RAM's). The PIAs are the 6520 of the PET, the RAM eight 4164s, the ROM the 27C128. It boots to the EXTENDED COLOR BASIC 1.1 prompt and takes the keyboard (the matrix is wired through the PIA's nets: capitals without Shift, Esc = BREAK, Home = CLEAR, Backspace or Left = left arrow, arrow keys). The 6847's character set and colours are from floooh's chips project (zlib licence, notice kept in `src/coco_chips.c`). Put the ROMs in `roms/coco2` (see the README). Not simulated yet: cassette, joysticks, sound, the serial port, cartridges, the disk controller, and the SAM's fast and address dependent speeds (the machine always runs at 0.89 MHz).

- **Apple ][+ (first release: motherboard, keyboard, composite video).** `--machine apple2plus` or `--board apple2plus`, or the new Apple tab. Built at pin level from the rev 0 motherboard schematic (redrawn in KiCad by baldengineer's "bit-preserve"), which `tools/apple2_sch2net.py` reads straight from the KiCad sheets into a netlist (`tools/apple2plus-netlist-src/`, then `tools/apple2plus_netlist2c.py` makes `src/apple2plus_board_data.c` and the part table): the 6502, 48K of 4116 DRAM in three rows of eight behind the 74LS153/257/283 address multiplexers, the six 2K ROMs, the 2716 character generator, and the board's own clock, video and decoding in 74LS TTL, all derived from the 14.31818 MHz crystal. It boots to the Applesoft `]` prompt (banner "APPLE ][") and takes the keyboard (capitals only; Esc, Backspace/Left, Right, Enter). The picture is the board's VIDEO and SYNC as a 14.31818 MHz dot stream, decoded in the monitor as NTSC (the text's colour fringes and the high resolution artifact colours come from that). The keyboard encoder is outside the board: the keyboard connector A7 is a part that holds a key's seven bits and strobe. Put the ROMs in `roms/apple2plus` (see the README). Not simulated yet: the Disk II card and slots, sound (the speaker toggle is wired), the cassette input, paddles (PDL reads 255) and the oscillator's transistors (the crystal drives the gate directly).

- **Commodore 128: CP/M's screen is black, not magenta.** The VDC's answer to a Z80 read was on the bus only for the clock before the Z80 took it, so the Z80 read $FF (the pulled-up bus) instead of the register: the start-up ROM's read-modify-write of the VDC's R24 (`IN A,(C)`, `AND $7F`, `OUT (C),A`) wrote $7F and set its reverse-screen bit, which turned CP/M's black-on-light-purple text into purple text on a purple page. The VDC now keeps a Z80 read's data out until the Z80 has latched it; CP/M shows light purple text on black, as its attribute byte says.
- **Commodore 128: fast serial, and five fixes for CP/M, which now reaches the A> prompt.** The drive now answers the C128 Kernal's fast-serial handshake (8 sync pulses on SRQ before a command; a byte clocked on SRQ in each direction), so the Kernal marks the 1581 as fast, the CP/M BIOS stops treating it as a 1541 and uses fast sector reads. Found on the way: in Z80 mode a memory read of $D500-$D50B was also answered by the MMU registers (they answer the Z80's I/O only), which broke the BIOS read routine that straddles $D500; the CIA's read-to-clear interrupt register was cleared by the first clock of a Z80 I/O read and returned 0 on the next, losing the "byte received" flag; and a DRAM write strobe lingering into the next Z80 cycle wrote the byte again at the next cycle's address (it overwrote BIOS code during the CCP.COM load). And a read of $FF00 returned the RAM's byte ANDed with the MMU's CR (two outputs on the bus, the low bits win), so the BIOS's save-and-restore of the CR around its calls into the start-up ROM put the Z80 in bank 0, where the CCP's stack read back as ROM and the boot started over; the RAM now stays off the bus while the MMU answers there. CP/M Plus boots from the ZPM system disk to the `A>` prompt. `DIRECTORY` from BASIC still works.
- **The right column can be resized.** Drag its left edge (the pointer turns into a left-right arrow there) to make the Monitor, Controls and Oscilloscope column wider or narrower; the monitor picture scales with it. It stays between 340 pixels and the window width less 320 for the board.
- **TS1000/ZX81: a RAM option, 2K or 1K.** The ZX81 had 1K and the TS1000 has 2K on the same board. The new *RAM* drop-down (next to the board choice) leaves A10 of the RAM chip out for 1K, so the 1K repeats at $4400 as on a real 1K machine; the ROM's own memory test then finds RAMTOP at $4400 (1K) or $4800 (2K). The default stays 2K. The change restarts the machine.
- **The TS1000 is labelled TS1000/ZX81** in the Timex board drop-down, since it is the ZX81 circuit.
- **Commodore 128: a Z80 I/O instruction no longer writes the RAM under the chip.** The Z80 core lets /IORQ go while the I/O address is still on the bus, so the DRAM took the tail of every `OUT` as a memory write: the start-up ROM's VDC writes (`OUT (C),A` with BC=$D600/$D601) wrote over CP/M's BIOS code at $D600, which made CP/M ask for "Insert Disk L in Drive A" and stop. The DRAM now ignores a write while the Z80 is between an I/O cycle and its next bus cycle. CP/M gets past that prompt and on to reading the directory; the A> prompt is not reached yet ("BIOS Err on A: No CCP.COM file").
- **Commodore 128: CP/M stays up, and a Disk... button.** CP/M Plus now runs past the boot banner instead of starting over: the Z80 sees RAM at $D000-$DFFF whatever the CR says (it reaches the chips by I/O), and the start-up ROM covers only the bottom 4K of bank 0, so CP/M's page zero (the `CALL 5` entry) in bank 1 is RAM. A new *Disk...* button (C128 only) opens a file dialog to put a `.d64`, `.d71` or `.d81` in drive 8, *Eject* takes it out again, and a small red box next to it lights while the drive is busy.
- **Commodore 128: a disk drive on the serial bus.** The serial connector (CN6) now has a drive behind it: drop a `.d64`, `.d71` or `.d81` on the window, or give it on the command line, and `DIRECTORY`, `LOAD` and `RUN` work through the real IEC bus (ATN, CLK, DATA, SRQ), with the 1581 burst commands (fast serial on SRQ) too. A disk is write protected for now (no `SAVE`). The 1581 CP/M disk's boot sector redirect is understood, so `BOOT` runs the CP/M boot sector, which loads the Z80 loader by burst reads. Also fixed: the Z80 had no common RAM (the 8722's RCR applies to it too), and the 8502 read a stale byte on its first cycle after the Z80 had the bus. CP/M Plus boots from the ZPM system disk with `BOOT`, to the "CP/M 3.0 on the C128" banner on the 80-column screen. More fixes for that: the 8722 ignores memory writes at $D5xx from the Z80 unless I/O is switched in (its registers answer the Z80's I/O instructions), the VDC does one access per Z80 I/O cycle and its block fill/copy count is exact.
- **Commodore 128: C64 mode.** A new button, *Mode: C128 / C64*, starts the machine the way the real one does it: switched off and on with the C= key held, so the Z80's start-up code sets the 8722 MMU to C64 mode and the 8502 runs the C64 BASIC V2 and Kernal from the system ROM, with the 8721 PLA acting as the C64's. It boots to "**** COMMODORE 64 BASIC V2 ****", takes the keyboard and shows sprites. `GO64` from C128 BASIC works too, and a reset returns to C128 mode.
- **Commodore 128: the 80-column screen.** The 8563 VDC draws its RGBI picture (text with attributes, reverse, underline, blink, the cursor, bitmap mode, 8 pixel and double-wide dots) from its registers and 16K of video RAM, and a new button under RESET, *Monitor: composite / RGBI*, plugs the monitor into the VIC's or the VDC's output, as you would move the cable. The button also sets the 40/80 DISPLAY key to match and restarts the machine (the Kernal reads the key at start-up only), so one click gives the 80-column screen and another gives back the 40-column one; F11 still works on its own, followed by RESET. The monitor picture is now 784 x 544 (the 40-column picture twice as big). Not done: the VDC's smooth scrolling, interlace modes and light pen.
- **Commodore 128, schematic 310378 rev 7A (first release: 40-column C128 mode).** `--machine c128`, `--board 310378` or the Commodore tab. Built at pin level from the schematic: the Z80A starts the machine from the 4K start-up ROM and hands over to the 8502 through the 8722 MMU (MCR write, /Z80EN, /BUSRQ), with the U12/U13 latches and glue gates; 8721 PLA (from the read-out in `tools/8721-pla.v`, corrected against the raw chip dump: the VIC's character fetch in 128 mode, the colour RAM address and one Z80 I/O term), 128K of DRAM in sixteen 4164s, the 8564 VIC-IIe 40-column picture, two 6526 CIAs, the keyboard (C64 matrix plus the extra keypad columns read through the VIC; the keymap window shows it, Page Down is TAB) and both joysticks. The board view puts the chips and connectors where they are on the real board, taken from Johan Grip's C128 Neo / Open128 KiCad layout (CC BY-SA 4.0); the capacitors and resistors are placed automatically. It boots to "COMMODORE BASIC V7.0 122365 BYTES FREE" and takes the keyboard. Put the ROMs in `roms/c128`. Not simulated yet: CP/M, SID sound, the expansion port and the serial bus beyond the wiring. Netlist: `tools/c128_board.py`.
- **Commodore Plus/4, schematic 310164.** The Plus/4 (`--machine plus4`, `--board 310164`, or the Commodore tab), built on the C16's TED, 7501 and PLA models: 64K of DRAM in eight 4164s (U11-U18) behind the 74LS257 multiplexers, four ROMs (BASIC, KERNAL and the two 3-plus-1 function ROMs on F1), the 6551 ACIA (registers only), the user-port 6529B and the 74LS175 ROM latch with the 74LS139 and 74LS27 selects. The netlist was typed from the schematic and the service manual (`tools/plus4_board.py`); placement is approximate. It boots to "COMMODORE BASIC V3.5 60671 BYTES FREE", takes the keyboard (same map as the C16, shown in the keymap window), both joysticks (CN5, CN6 with the diodes CR11-CR20) read correctly through JOY(), and F1 starts the 3-PLUS-1 ROM. Put the ROMs in `roms/plus4`. Sound, analog video and double clock speed are not simulated; the cassette and serial bus are wired but untested.
- **Fixed:** the 74LS175 model had Q4 and /Q4 (pins 15 and 14) swapped. The TED now treats $FC00-$FCFF as RAM when the ROMs are switched off (BASIC keeps strings there); this does not change the C16 boot (12277 bytes free).
- **Commodore 16, ASSY 250443 (PCB 251789, schematic 251788).** The C16 (`--machine c16`, `--board 251788`, or the Commodore tab): the 7501 CPU, the 7360 TED (a new model: it makes the system clock from the 14.31818 MHz crystal, the DRAM strobes /RAS, MUX and /CAS, the ROM selects /CS0 and /CS1, AEC and BA, does the character DMA and the refresh, and draws the 40 x 25 text, extended colour, multicolour and bitmap screens; its timers, raster interrupt, keyboard latch ($FF08) and the ROM on/off switch ($FF3E, $FF3F) work; the sound, the analog video outputs, the light pen and the double clock speed are not simulated), the 7700-010 PLA (from its truth table), the 6529B keyboard port, the 74LS175 ROM latch, two 4416s behind the 74LS257 address multiplexers, and the two 23128 ROMs. The netlist was typed from the three sheets of the schematic and the service manual (`tools/c16_board.py`); the chips and connectors are placed as in a photo of the board, the passives near their chips. It boots to "COMMODORE BASIC V3.5 12277 BYTES FREE" and takes the keyboard (Esc = RUN/STOP, Insert = ESC, Tab = CTRL, Left Alt = C=, F4 = HELP); programs (`.prg`, BASIC at $1001) load and RUN. The ROMs are not included: put `basic.318006-01.bin` and `kernal.318005-04.bin` in `roms/c16`. The two joysticks work (the keypad 8 2 4 6 and 0 for fire is port 1; PgUp, PgDn, Del, End and Right Ctrl is port 2; the select lines, the diodes D1-D10 and the EMI filters are simulated, and BASIC's JOY() reads them); the keyboard map window shows the C16's keys. The cassette and the serial bus are wired but have nothing to talk to.
- **The Spectrum 48K ROM is a file too.** `chips/zx48-rom.h` is gone: the 16K ROM (the image of IC5, SHA-1 5ea7c2b824672e914525d1d5c419d71b84a426a2) is read from `roms/zx48` (any 16K .rom or .bin there), or from `roms` or the current folder when the name has zx48, 48k or spectrum in it. A missing ROM shows in the status line. **Put the ROM in `roms/zx48` after pulling, or the Spectrum boards will not start.**
- **The C64 ROMs are files, like the VIC-20's and the PET's.** The BASIC, KERNAL and character ROM images are no longer compiled into the program (`chips/c64-roms.h` is gone): they are read from `roms/c64` (or `roms`, or the folder you start from) at start: BASIC 901226-01, KERNAL 901227-03 and characters 901225-01, or for the 250469 the 16K BASIC + KERNAL image 251913-01. A missing ROM shows in the status line. **If you build from a checkout, put your ROM files in `roms/c64` or the C64 will not start.** The images are Commodore's and are not in the repository any more.
- **VIC-20 key map.** The keyboard map window (the C64's layout, with its PC keys: Tab = CTRL, Left Alt = C=, Esc = RUN/STOP, Page Up = RESTORE) now also opens on both VIC-20 boards; the host keys follow it.
- **Commodore VIC-20, ASSY 250403.** The cost reduced VIC-20 board (`--board 250403`, `--machine vic20b`, or the Commodore tab): 6502, two 6522s, the 6560 (a new model: it divides the 14.31818 MHz crystal, drives the video bus in phase 1, fetches the matrix, colour RAM and characters and draws the 22 x 23 screen, registers $9000-$900F; sound, the analog video outputs, paddles and light pen are not simulated), 2114s and 6116s, the three 74LS138 decoders, the 74LS245 bus switches and the 7406. The netlist and the layout come from jimbro1000's KiCad project of the 250403 (the "Vicky Twenty" replica), with two fixes found by booting it: the 6502 address line A11 and the block select BLK4 were one net, and both 6522s were selected by A4. The ROMs are not included: put the BASIC (901486-01), KERNAL (901486-04) and character (901460-03) images in `roms/vic20`; a KERNAL dump one byte short (8191 bytes) is padded with $FF. It boots to the READY screen and takes the keyboard (Esc = RUN/STOP, Page Up = RESTORE). 
- **Commodore VIC-20, the early board (324001, MDK 25HB).** The VIC-20 with eleven 2114s (`--board 324001`, `--machine vic20a`): the colour RAM UE1 and five pairs UD2/UE2 ($0000), UD3/UE3 ... UD6/UE6 ($1000-$1FFF) on the outputs 0 and 4-7 of the 74LS138 UC4 (schematic 324001 sheet c). The circuit is the 250403's with that memory, plus the glue logic read from sheets a and c (`tools/vic20a_from_b.py`): the crystal oscillator and cross-coupled phase latch on the 7402 UB9, and the pin allocation of the 74LS02 UC3, 74LS04 UC2 and 7406 UB4, which differ from the 250403's. The rest of sheets a and b (VIAs, decoders, 6502) agree with the 250403 where I checked; the passives keep the 250403's references and values. The chips and connectors are placed as in the photo of the 25HB board; the passives are put next to the chip they connect to, not where the photo has them. Boots to the READY screen like the 250403.
- **A Commodore tab.** The C64 and the PET are one tab, Commodore, with the machine (PET 4032, PET 8032, C64 250407, 250425, 250469) in the drop-down; the tabs are now Commodore, Spectrum and Timex.
- **The .exe's icon with `make`.** `mingw32-make` now embeds the icon (src/icon.rc, src/c64-hw-sim.ico) as `build.bat` does; before, only `build.bat` did.
- **Chip types on the chips.** On every machine the logic, buffer, clock and glue chips are now named by their type (74LS00, 74LS157,
  74LS244, 4066, 556, 8701, 7406...) instead of a function word (GLUE, MUX, BUF, NAND, SW, INV...); the function is in the lines under
  the name when you zoom in. CPUs, VIC/SID/CIAs, ROMs and RAMs keep their names (CPU, VIC, BASIC, RAM1...).

- **C64 ASSY 250425.** A third C64 board (`--board 250425`, `--machine c64b`, or the board drop-down): the 250407 with the clock PLL
  (U29, U30, U32, the 74LS629 and the loop parts) replaced by the 8701 clock generator at U31, from schematic 251469. Generated from the
  250407 tables by `tools/c64_250425_from_250407.py`; netlist checked against the schematic (U27 gates, crystal trimmer, R31 differ from the 250407); layout is the 250407's with the PLA, SID, VIC, 8701, Y1, CT1 and C58 placed from a photo of a 250425.

- **PET chiclet keyboard.** The 4032 has a `keyboard` option (`--option keyboard=graphics|chiclet`, or the control panel): the 2001's chiclet
  keyboard, from a photo. It has the graphics keyboard's matrix, so only the Keyboard window changes (key places and legends, RETURN two
  rows tall); typing works as before.

- **TS1500 placement.** The TS1500's parts are placed from a photo of the board; the buck regulator and wires on it are mods and are not drawn.

- **Sinclair ZX80.** A seventh machine (`--machine zx80`, Timex in the control panel): Z80A and discrete TTL (no ULA), 2 x 2114
  (1K), the character latch, address multiplexers, line counter, video shift register and sync logic, from the ZX80 circuit
  diagram (netlist read from the vector PDF, `tools/zx80-extract/`). Two ROMs: the ZX80's own 4K (`roms/zx80/`) or the ZX81's 8K
  (`roms/ts1000/`), chosen with `--option rom=zx80|zx81` or the ROM button (the ZX81 ROM runs in FAST mode: no NMI generator).
  Parts placed from a scan of an Issue 3 board (resistor groups in order, not read from the silkscreen).
  Its keyboard is the ZX80's own (same matrix as the ZX81, different shifted keys: NOT AND THEN TO, HOME, EDIT on SHIFT+NEWLINE, " $ ( ) * on SHIFT+Y U I O P), also in the Keyboard window; `--option keyboard=zx81` (or the control panel) switches to the ZX81's keys.
- **Timex Sinclair 1500.** A sixth machine (`--machine ts1500`, Timex in the control panel): Z80A, the 68 pin gate array U5
  (the ZX81 ULA's logic, reused from `src/ula81.c`, plus its own DRAM timing), 8K ROM, eight 4116 DRAMs (16K), the
  74LS244/245 buffers, keyboard diodes and edge connector. Boots to the K cursor headless and runs `PRINT 1`. The netlist
  is transcribed from the schematic "TS-1500 Rev 1.0, redrawn 2015" (Dick Berry), which prints every pin of U5; ten of
  them (the Z80 control lines) were also checked by continuity on a real board and agree. The refs are the real ones (Z80
  U7, keyboard J5 / J6, diodes D10-D17, R25 and R28 in the ROM lines, U6 DIR on /WR). The video, MIC and EAR corner is
  transcribed (passives only). The ROM is not shipped: put it in `roms/ts1500`. The Keyboard window shows the TS1000 keys.

- **Timex Sinclair 1000 (ZX81), Issue 3.** A fifth machine (`--machine ts1000`, Timex in the control panel): Z80A,
  ULA (a functional model, `src/ula81.c`), 8K ROM, 2K RAM, the buffer resistors, the /WAIT and clock transistors,
  keyboard diodes and the edge connector. Boots to the K cursor headless and runs `PRINT 1`. The netlist is
  transcribed from the schematic SRC 049 (re-drawn by Ron Reuter); what the schematic doesn't say (the order of the
  R35-R47 pull-ups, the keyboard and modulator connector pins) is marked INFERRED in `tools/ts1000-netlist-src/sheet01.py`.
  Placement is from a scan of an Issue 3 board. The 8K ROM is not shipped: put it in `roms/ts1000`.
  The Keyboard window has the ZX81 keys (RUBOUT is the same as DELETE).
- **Keyboard map.** A Keyboard button in the controls opens a window with the machine's keys and the
  PC key that types each one (`src/keymap_view.c`): the Spectrum and TS2068 (keywords, SYMBOL SHIFT
  legends), the C64 (physical layout), the PET 4032 and 8032 (the editor ROM's key table, in scan order).
- **Timex Sinclair TS2068.** The TS2068 PC board 335-850001-03 as a fourth machine
  (`--machine ts2068`, TS2068 in the control panel): Z80A, home ROM and EXROM, 4416 DRAMs,
  AY-3-8912, keyboard and joystick diodes, dock connector, and a model of the SCLD with
  its display fetch and contended clock. Boots to the BASIC screen headless; netlist
  from the Technical Manual schematic, placement from its layout drawing. The SCLD
  timing is assumed. No tape, file loading or sound yet; a 03D board's patch board is not
  modelled. Written by Claude, directed by David Green (daver99999). See `docs/TS2068.md`.
  The ROMs are not included.
- The program is now built as `8BitHWSimulator` (`8BitHWSimulator.exe`).
- **Commodore PET.** The 8032080 universal dynamic board, as a 4032 (40
  columns, graphics keyboard) and an 8032 (80 columns, business keyboard), both
  in the same program: pick PET in the control panel, then the board (`--board
  8032080-40` or `8032080-80`). Pin level models of the 6520, 6522 and 6545 (new
  cores, `chips/m6520.h`, `m6522.h`, `m6545.h`, with unit tests in
  `tests/chips/`), the 4116 and 2114 RAMs, the ROM sockets, the glue logic and the
  power section, with the chips where they sit on the real board. Programs
  (`.prg`, `.pet`) load and run; ROMs are found in `roms/pet` by part number or
  opened from the file browser; RESET button. Netlist transcribed from Commodore's
  logic diagram 8032081 rev E (`tools/pet-netlist-src/`, rebuilt by
  `tools/pet_netlist2c.py`), placement read from the assembly drawing 8032080 rev
  J. Written by Claude, directed and tested by David Green (daver99999). See
  `docs/PET.md`. The PET ROMs are not included.
- The fault atlas and the diagnosis tools do not cover the PET; the Diagnose
  section says so instead of failing.
- `board_part_info` and the other part accessors cope with a part that has no
  description (a chip with none used to crash the board drawing).
- The Makefile links `winmm` on Windows (`timeBeginPeriod`), as build.bat already did.
- Builds with any MinGW toolchain again (MSYS2, TDM-GCC, older mingw-w64),
  not just w64devkit: win_perf.h now looks up its Windows 10 power and
  CPU-set functions at run time instead of needing headers that declare
  them ("implicit declaration of function 'SetThreadInformation'").
  The .exe also starts on Windows 7/8 again, where those calls are skipped.

## V1.3 (2026-11-11)

- Continuity: the readout names what drives the net tested, from what
  each pin drove this frame ("Driven by U7.40"), and triangles on the

  can't. It can also ask for one leg when only that tells the faults left
  apart. Uses the atlas's new leg readings (LEGS), in the atlas that
  comes with this release.
- A link or part that isn't fitted now says why when clicked: which option
  in the controls fits it, or that the board has no link there.
- Diagnose from a screenshot: the screen's edge is found in unevenly lit
  pictures too (a phone photo, brighter in the middle), where the whole
  picture used to be taken for the screen. A blurred screen edge, half
  border color, is no longer taken for something drawn on the screen.
- The menu of a chip's pin is wide enough for its longest choices
  ("Output dead inside the chip, stuck high" was cut off).
- Chip view: an arrow inside each pin shows which way it goes (into the
  chip: an input, out of it: an output, a diamond: both ways; none for
  power and analog pins).
- Pins wrongly called outputs are fixed: on the 74LS629 oscillators (U31)
  the frequency, range, capacitor and OSCVCC pins, on the 556 timer (U20)
  the THR and CV pins, the 8701's crystal pins, the LM1889's analog pins
  and some unused (NC) pins. Analog pins now say "analog" when pointed at.
  The atlas build drops the faults this removes ("output dead" on those
  pins) when run with C64HW_ATLAS_PRUNE=1.
- The fault atlas is corrected: 316 Spectrum faults (Issues 3B, 2, 1 and
  4B) had screens from an older version of the simulator, so a screenshot
  or probe diagnosis could suggest the wrong fault for them: 274 open pins
  and 42 solder bridges, mostly on the address multiplexers IC3 and IC4.
  It also has the new leg readings for probing on the chips' legs.
